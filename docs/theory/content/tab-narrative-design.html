Narrative Design

Narrative Design is the second editorial planning page inside Book Settings. It translates the story foundation into conflict logic, macro structure, escalation, and storytelling constraints.

Workflow Position

Use Narrative Design after Story Core and before Blueprint. It prepares the narrative logic that Blueprint later expands into beats and pacing.

Section Order

  1. Core Conflict
  2. Macro Structure
  3. Conflict Escalation
  4. Narrative Constraints

Core Conflict

This section defines the story's primary struggle and the broad shape of its ending.

  • Central Conflict - the main struggle of the story
  • Resolution Path - victory, sacrifice, tragic fall, or bittersweet ending

Macro Structure

This section sets the book-level structural skeleton.

  • Structure Model - narrative arc selected from the available work-level arcs
  • Chapters - macro chapter list with title, turning point, and role
  • Turning Points - inciting incident, midpoint, climax, resolution, and related structure beats
  • Use Arc Template - applies reusable narrative arc templates directly into Macro Structure

Use the chapter list to sketch the high-level movement of the book before moving to Story Map or Manuscript.

Narrative Arc Templates open in a large template browser and can prefill structure model, chapter roles, turning points, and tension direction before manual refinement.

Conflict Escalation

This section controls how pressure grows over time.

  • Conflict Type - the broad mode of struggle
  • Escalation Pattern - wave, spiral, linear, and related growth shapes
  • Stakes Growth - how the consequences expand from personal to wider scales

Narrative Constraints

This section defines storytelling conditions rather than plot structure.

  • Non-linear timeline
  • Moral ambiguity
  • Multiple POV
  • Unreliable narrator

Relationship to Other Pages

  • Story Core provides theme, wisdom, and character transformation
  • Narrative Design turns that foundation into conflict and structure logic
  • Blueprint then expands the logic into beats, key events, pacing, and tension

Best Practices

  1. Write Central Conflict as a concrete struggle, not a vague topic.
  2. Choose one clear Structure Model before adding many chapters.
  3. Use chapter roles to define function, not summary.
  4. Reserve Narrative Constraints for storytelling choices that materially affect delivery.
